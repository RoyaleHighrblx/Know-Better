PAUSE. CHECK. VERIFY.

Myth detective

Online health claims can be confident without being accurate. Use a repeatable method to evaluate what you see before trusting or sharing it.

1. Identify the source

Find the original author or organization. A repost may leave out important context.

2. Look for evidence

Does the author explain how they know? Are reliable sources linked and represented fairly?

3. Check the date

Some information changes. Check when it was published or last reviewed.

4. Compare and verify

Look for agreement across independent, reputable health organizations. One post is not enough.

School support

Knowing who can help is an important part of health literacy. School-specific contact information can be added here after an authorized school setup process is available.

For schools: configure before sharing with students.

The information below is a general starting point, not a directory for a specific school. A participating school should verify staff roles, office locations, contact methods, and emergency procedures with its administration.

School nurse

Can help with health-related questions at school and explain the school's process for getting appropriate support.

Ask where the health office is located.

School counselor

Can listen and help students navigate school concerns, friendships, emotions, and available support.

Ask how students can make an appointment.

Trusted school adult

A teacher, coach, or staff member can help a student find the right person to speak with.

Identify an adult you can approach.

If you are worried about someone's immediate safety

Tell a trusted adult or school staff member right away. If there is an immediate emergency, contact local emergency services. Follow your school's approved safety procedures.
